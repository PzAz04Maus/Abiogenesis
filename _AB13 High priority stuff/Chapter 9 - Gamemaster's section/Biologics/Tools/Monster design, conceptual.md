### Tool use (Prehensility)

- Prehensility
	- precision (level of tool use) - level of skill in fine control and manipulation, like using a pencil or screwdriver.
	- power - the ability to transfer force or lift a tool. In most cases, opposing arms do not count in a power grip. 

A 2 factor stat, $\frac{precision}{power}$ is a coefficient that rates the transfer of a creatures overall dexterity or strength to their digits. Goes from 0 to A(10). A is human hand equivalent, 0 is nonexistent. 

- Mouths tend to have a reasonably high power grip.
- Prehensile tongues have a reasonable power grip
- Elephant trunks have a strong power grip and near human precision. While they can pick up single blades of grass they do not have an opposing thumb
- Octopus Tentacles are a bit of a special case because their power grip is facilitated by suckers, but they have a human equivalent power grip or better and a near equivalent precision grip with the potential to form opposable forces with the same tentacle.
- Squid tentacles only have an equivalent power grip.
- Partially prehensile tails have a low power grip and no precision
    - rats
    - capuchin monkey
- Prehensile tails have a high power grip but little precision
    - some snakes
    - most possums
    - many new world monkeys

I remember having an in depth conversation about this with a biologist. They were saying a super intelligent alien would probably have to be able to live long lives and have a really long infancy to develop a mind like that. If you look at a dog that's 3 months old compared to a baby that's 3 months old, the dog can already function and can start learning basic commands. The human mind being more complex takes a lot longer to develop, so it seems reasonable that if there are super intelligent beings they could potentially have those limitations. Just a fun thought I've not seen anyone mention before.
