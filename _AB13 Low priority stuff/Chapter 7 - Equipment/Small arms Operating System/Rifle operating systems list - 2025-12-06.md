# 🔧 **Rifle Operating Systems (Specific, Named Types)**

## **1. Gas-Operated Systems**

### **A. Direct Impingement (True DI)**
Gas vents directly against the bolt carrier (no piston).
- **Stoner Direct Impingement** – AR-15/M16 family  
- **Ljungman AG-42 / Hakim DI System** – true “direct jet” impingement  
- **MAS-49/49-56 DI System**

---

### **B. Gas Piston – Long-Stroke**
Piston and bolt carrier move as a rigid assembly.
- **Kalashnikov Long-Stroke Piston** – AK-47/AKM/AK-74  
- **M1 Garand Long-Stroke Piston**  
- **SKS Long-Stroke Piston**  
- **Galil Long-Stroke (AK-derived)**  
- **SIG 510 (Stgw 57) Roller-delayed + LS piston hybrid**

---

### **C. Gas Piston – Short-Stroke (Tappet)**
Piston gives a momentary strike to the carrier.
- **Garand-Tappet Short Stroke (M1 Carbine)**  
- **HK G36 Short Stroke Piston**  
- **FN SCAR Short Stroke Piston**  
- **SIG MCX/516 Short Stroke Piston**  
- **Armalite AR-18/AR-180 Short-Stroke Piston**  
- **FAMAS F1 (lever-delayed) + short-stroke piston hybrid**

---

### **D. Gas Trap**
Early system capturing muzzle gas without barrel ports.
- **Bang Gas Trap** – M1922, early M1 Garand prototypes  
- **German G41(W)**  
- **Japanese Type 5 Prototype**

---

## **2. Recoil-Operated Systems**

### **A. Long Recoil**
Barrel and bolt recoil together full distance; then separate.
- **Browning Auto-5** (shotgun, but foundational)  
- **Remington Model 8 / Model 81** (recoil-operated rifles)

### **B. Short Recoil**
Barrel and bolt recoil together briefly before unlocking.
- **FG-42 Short Recoil System**  
- **KRISS Vector (for SMGs)** – unusual but SR derived  
- **Some early Mannlicher automatic rifle prototypes**

---

## **3. Delayed-Blowback Systems (Non-Gas)**

### **A. Roller-Delayed Blowback**
Rollers slow bolt movement; gas not used.
- **HK G3 / HK91**  
- **HK33**  
- **CETME Model B/C/L**

---

### **B. Lever-Delayed Blowback**
Mechanical lever slows bolt.
- **FAMAS F1/F2**  
- **AAI / Chyteck prototypes**  
- **French AAT 52 (LMG)** uses a variation

---

### **C. Gas-Delayed Blowback**
Gas tapped to delay bolt opening, but no piston.
- **HK P7** (pistol, classic example)  
- **Steyr GB** (pistol; rifle examples are rare)  
- **Heckler & Koch G11 caseless is *conceptually adjacent* but distinct**  

(Rifles rarely use this because of heat and fouling.)

---

### **D. Chamber-Fluted or Adhesion-Delayed Blowback**
Surface treatments assist delayed opening.
- **HK G11 Caseless System** (uses flutes to manage adhesion)  
- **CETME early experiments**

---

## **4. Straight Blowback (Simple Blowback)**
Bolt mass + spring oppose cartridge pressure; no locking.
Mostly pistol-caliber carbines, but included for completeness.
- **Hi-Point Carbines**  
- **Sterling SMG**  
- **PPSh-41** (SMG-level, but rifle-length examples are uncommon)

---

## **5. Other / Exotic Rifle Operating Systems**

### **A. Gas-Boosted Recoil (“Gas Assist”)**
Small gas impulse assists recoil operation.
- **Johnson LMG variants**  
- **FG-42 (hybrid recoil system includes gas assist)**

### **B. Constant-Recoil Systems**
Designed so the bolt carrier never impacts rear trunnion.
- **Ultimax 100** (LMG, but influential design principle)

### **C. Caseless / Non-Conventional Propulsion**
- **HK G11 Rotary-Chamber System**  

### **D. Toggle-Locked (Delayed)**
- **Schwarzlose MG**  
- **Pedersen rifle toggle-delayed prototypes**  
- **Swiss Furrer rifles & LMGs** (notorious complexity)

# 🔧 **Gas Piston System Sub-Types (Detailed Engineering Families)**

## **1. Long-Stroke Gas Piston: Important Subtypes**

Long-stroke means the **piston and bolt carrier travel together** for the full rearward motion.

### **1A. Rigid Long-Stroke Piston**
Piston is fixed to the bolt carrier; moves as one solid mass.
- **Kalashnikov AK family**  
- **SKS**  
- **SIG SG 510 (StG 57)** — but with roller-delayed bolt head  
- **Galil / Valmet**

**Key trait:** Heavy reciprocating mass → smooth impulse, good reliability, but more sight disruption.

---

### **1B. Sliding Long-Stroke Rod (Non-rigid coupling)**
Piston is *not directly fixed* to the carrier; uses a cam or bearing surface.
- **M1 Garand**  
- **M14**  
- **ZB-26 / Bren LMG lineage**  
- **M1941 Johnson LMG (variant)**

**Key trait:** The **op-rod** is a separate guided mass →  
- softer impulse than rigid AK-type  
- more prone to bending or op-rod stress (M14 especially)

**This is why the Garand action is its own recognizable subtype.**

---

### **1C. Annular Long-Stroke Piston**
Piston rings around the barrel, usually concentric.
- **VSSK Vykhlop experimental forms**  
- Rare in rifles, more common in shotguns and prototypes.

---

---

# 🔧 **2. Short-Stroke Gas Piston: Subtypes**

Short-stroke means the piston delivers a **brief impulse** (“tap”) then stops, letting the carrier continue.

There are **three major engineering families**:

---

## **2A. Tappet Piston (Hammer/Tappet Type)**
A small piston slams into the carrier and *rebounds off*.
- **M1 Carbine (classic tappet)**  
- **ArmaLite AR-18 / AR-180**  
- **HK G36**  
- **SIG MCX / SIG 516**  
- **FN SCAR (variant)**

**Characteristics:** simple, light, compact; the piston does not follow the carrier.

---

## **2B. Sliding Short-Stroke Rod (Push-Rod)**
A longer rod pushes the carrier for a short distance before stopping.
- **FNC**  
- **Daewoo K2**  
- **FN MAG / M240** (MG but representative)  
- **Japanese Type 89**

**Traits:**  
- gentler impulse than tappet  
- allows tuned dwell timing  
- intermediate between AR-18 and FAL dynamics

---

## **2C. Annular Short-Stroke Piston**
Gas acts on a **ring-shaped piston** surrounding the barrel, pushing a collar.
- **FN FAL (modernized variants)**  
- **AR-15 piston retrofit kits (Adams Arms, HK416 style)**  
- **HK416** (annular piston + very short travel)  
- **AUG (Steyr AUG uses a rotary-driven annular piston group)**

**Traits:**  
- excellent alignment  
- reduces carrier tilt  
- minimizes side loads on op-rods  

This is the most “modern” short-stroke architecture.

---

---

# 🔧 **3. Direct Impingement Subtypes (Rarely Listed, but Real)**

Though often treated as one category, DI systems have meaningful variations:

## **3A. Internal Gas Expansion (True DI)**
Gas *expands inside the bolt carrier* and creates a differential pressure.
- **Stoner AR-15**  
- **Stoner 63 (in DI configuration)**

Carrier acts like a piston with gas rings — internally.

---

## **3B. Jet Impingement (AG-42 Type)**
A jet of gas strikes a surface on the carrier directly.
- **Ljungman AG-42**  
- **Hakim**

This is mechanically simpler but harsher.

---

## **3C. Impingement-on-Operating-Rod (Hybrid)**
Gas jets push a small internal “floating rod.”
- **French MAS-49 / 49-56**  
(not usually called a piston, but mechanically similar)

---

---

# 🔧 **4. Locking & Integration Subtypes (Cutting Across Gas Types)**

These aren’t “gas systems” themselves but create recognizable **action families** tied to the gas mechanism.

## **4A. Tilting Bolt Gas-Operated**
Bolt tilts down/up to lock.
- **FN FAL** (short-stroke annular piston + tilting bolt)  
- **SKS** (long-stroke + tilting bolt)  
- **CZ Bren 2 (tilting bolt heritage)**  
- **Tokarev SVT-40**

---

## **4B. Rotating Bolt Gas-Operated**
Bolt head rotates via cam path.
- **AR-15 (DI)**  
- **AK (long-stroke piston)**  
- **G36 (short-stroke piston)**  
- **SCAR**  
- **SIG 550** (short-stroke rod + rotating bolt)

---

## **4C. Flapper Locking Gas-Operated**
Bolt uses flaps to lock into recesses.
- **Degtyarev DP-28** (long-stroke piston + flapper lock)  
- **Japanese Type 11 / 96 / 99 LMGs**

---

---

# 📌 **Comparison: Garand vs Kalashnikov (Why They’re Distinct Subtypes)**

### **Garand Action (Garand/M14 family):**
- **Sliding long-stroke op-rod** (not rigid)  
- **Separate op-rod + piston head**  
- **Rotating bolt with dual locking lugs**  
- Gas port timing tied to a long op-rod  
- Op-rod curves → sensitive to stress & harmonics

### **Kalashnikov Action (AK family):**
- **Rigid long-stroke piston permanently fixed to carrier**  
- **Massive, simple, highly tolerant to fouling**  
- Straight-line piston → very robust  
- Bolt rotates via cam path in the carrier  
- Carrier + piston act as one heavy impulse system

These differences create the two **primary sub-families of long-stroke systems** used in the 20th century.

# 🔍 **ENGINEERING SUBTYPES EXPLAINED (WITH EXAMPLES)**

Below is the detailed reasoning behind each class — similar depth to the Garand vs AK explanation earlier.

---

## **1. Simple Blowback**

The bolt mass + recoil spring resist case pressure.

### **A. Straight Blowback**
- Uzi, MAC-10, MP40, Sterling, Beretta M12  
- Rugged, cheap, but requires heavy bolt.

### **B. Telescoping Bolt Blowback**
Bolt shrouds the barrel → allows shorter guns.
- Uzi (archetype)  
- B&T APC9  
- CZ Scorpion Evo 3 (refined version)

---

## **2. Mass-Delayed Blowback**

Uses moving internal mass or inertia to resist early opening.

### **A. Inertia-Locked**
- Benelli pistols (rare)  
- Relies on bolt head inertia.

### **B. Inertia-Augmented / Moving-Mass Delay**
- FAMAE SAF  
- Internal parts slide to delay unlocking.

### **C. Rotating-Mass Delay**
- Peruvian MGP-84 SMGs  
- Bolt rotation adds delay.

---

## **3. Mechanically Delayed Blowback**

### **A. Roller-Delayed Blowback (RDB)**
- MP5 (benchmark example)  
- CETME pistols, prototypes  
- Provides smooth recoil and precise timing.

### **B. Lever-Delayed Blowback**
- SIG MKPS prototypes  
- AAT-52 lineage  
- Allows lighter bolt mass.

### **C. Radial-Delayed Blowback (Angular Cam Delay)**
- CMMG RDB PCCs  
- Bolt rotates slightly against a cam path before moving rearward.

### **D. Cam-Path / Helical Delay**
- Kriss Vector (mass moves downward + cam delays opening)  
- Savage 1907/1915 internal helical delay  
- Swiss MKMO variants  

### **E. Hesitation-Lock (Pedersen Action)**
- Remington 51, R51  
- Bolt “pauses” on a locking shoulder.

### **F. Toggle-Delayed**
- Luger pistol (famous example)  
- Some Swiss SMG prototypes  
- Articulated knee-joint mechanism delays opening.

---

## **4. Gas-Delayed / Gas-Retarded Blowback**

Gas tapped into a cylinder slows bolt movement.

- HK P7  
- Steyr GB  
- Walther CCP  

Not common in SMGs due to heating.

---

## **5. Gas Piston Systems in Pistol Caliber Guns**

### **A. Short-Stroke Piston**
- SIG MPX  
- LWRC SMG-45  
- Some AR-type PCCs using piston conversions

### **B. Long-Stroke Piston**
- Extremely rare in PCCs  
- Seen in experimental SIG MKPS  
- Some modern Russian PDWs in 9×39

Pistons make sense mainly for suppressed fire where blowback gets too dirty.

---

## **6. Special / Exotic Systems**

### **A. KRISS Vector Mass-Shift System**
A unique delay method where bolt mass moves **downward**, reducing recoil and delaying breech opening. It's its own entire category.

### **B. Gas-Seal Revolver System**
Nagant M1895: cylinder moves forward to seal the gap — an unusual gas system in a pistol-caliber weapon.

### **C. Constant-Recoil or Counter-Recoil Concepts**
Rare, but important in experimental SMG designs.

# 🔍 **DETAILED ENGINEERING SUBTYPES**

## **1. Turnbolt Actions (Conventional Bolt-Actions)**

### **1A. Two-Lug Front-Locking (Mauser Pattern)**
**Examples:** Mauser 98, Win M70, Rem 700, Tikka T3  
**Traits:**
- 90° bolt lift  
- Strong primary extraction cam  
- Extremely strong locking  
- Dominant military design of 20th century  
- Can handle magnum pressures (good barrel tenon engagement)

---

### **1B. Three-Lug Front-Locking (“Fast-Lift” Modern Pattern)**
**Examples:** Sako, Weatherby, AI AW, many modern customs  
**Traits:**
- 60° bolt lift for faster cycling  
- Stiffer lockup due to lug symmetry  
- Often smoother but can have weaker primary extraction  
- Favored in precision rifles

---

### **1C. Rear-Locking Lugs (Lee-Enfield)**
**Examples:** SMLE, No.4, P14/M1917 (hybrid)  
**Traits:**
- Extremely fast cycling  
- Action “flexes,” which is acceptable for .303 pressures  
- High rate of fire (WWI trench performance)  
- Not ideal for high-pressure modern cartridges

---

### **1D. Interrupted or Threaded Lug Systems**
**Examples:** Ross Mk II/III  
**Traits:**
- Very smooth when clean  
- Catastrophic when reassembled incorrectly  
- Uses interrupted threaded lockup similar to artillery breech

---

---

# **2. Straight-Pull Action Subtypes**

### **2A. Cam-Track Rotating-Bolt Straight Pulls**
**Examples:** Merkel Helix, Savage Impulse, Strasser RS14  
**Mechanics:**  
Bolt head rotates via helical cam path as user pulls straight back.  
**Traits:**  
- Fast cycling  
- Secure lockup  
- More complex than turnbolts

---

### **2B. Expanding Collet Locking**
**Examples:** Blaser R93, R8  
**Mechanics:**  
Bolt head uses a radial collet that expands into a circular recess.  
**Traits:**  
- Very fast  
- Smooth  
- Requires precision machining  
- Catastrophic failure mode if not properly designed (rare but real)

---

### **2C. Ball-Bearing Locking (Heym SR30)**
**Mechanics:**  
Spherical bearings expand outward to lock into receiver.  
**Traits:**  
- Silent and smooth  
- Very strong  
- Very different from lug-based systems

---

### **2D. Schmidt–Rubin / K31 Type**
**Examples:** Swiss K31, 1911, 1889  
**Mechanics:**  
Bolt sleeve cams rotate the head while the bolt handle simply moves linearly.  
**Traits:**  
- Extremely smooth  
- Beautiful engineering  
- Lugs located **mid-bolt**, not at front or rear  
- Very accurate actions

# 🔍 **SUBTYPE EXPLANATIONS (ENGINEERING LEVEL)**

## ⭐ **I. Simple Blowback**

### Straight Blowback  
- Slide held closed by spring + mass (PPK, Makarov).  
- Least complex, most sensitive to cartridge pressure.

### Telescoping Blowback  
- Slide wraps around the barrel (mostly SMGs).  
- Rare in true pistols because barrel length is too short.

---

## ⭐ **II. Mass-Delayed Blowback**

### Inertia-Delayed  
- Benelli B76 uses momentum of slide against fixed breechblock.  
- Light recoil but requires specific timing.

### Slide-Mass Augmented  
- Astra 400 family.  
- Slide inertia provides delay → heavy slide required.

---

## ⭐ **III. Mechanically Delayed Blowback**

### Roller-Delayed  
- HK P9S is the only major pistol.  
- Very soft recoil, expensive to make.

### Lever-Delayed  
- Rare and experimental.

### Hesitation-Lock (Pedersen Lock)  
- Remington Model 51, R51.  
- Breech “pauses” on a locking shoulder → extremely soft impulse.

### Toggle-Delayed  
- Luger P08.  
- Iconic knee-joint toggle mechanism.

### Radial-Delayed  
- Savage 1907/1915/1917 → rotating barrel delays rearward travel.  
- Precursor to some modern rotary-barrel designs.

### Cam-Path or Mass-Shift Delay  
- KRISS Vector pistols and machine pistols.  
- Mass shifts downward to delay recoil.

---

## ⭐ **IV. Gas-Delayed Blowback**

### HK P7  
- Gas trapped under piston slows slide.  
- Extremely accurate but runs very hot.

### Steyr GB  
- Twin gas rings act as a retarder.

### Walther CCP  
- Modernized small-format gas-retarded pistol.

---

## ⭐ **V. Short-Recoil Pistols (The Dominant Modern System)**

### Browning Link System  
- M1911.  
- Barrel tilts via swinging link.

### Linkless Tilting-Barrel (SIG/Browning)  
- Glock, SIG P226/P320, FNX, HK USP/VP9.  
- The modern standard: cam block replaces link.

### Rotating-Barrel Short-Recoil  
- Beretta PX4, Beretta Cougar, Grand Power K100.  
- Reduced muzzle flip; inherently smooth.

### Swing-Barrel  
- Boberg XR9.  
- Exotic “reverse feeding” system.

### Falling-Block Short Recoil  
- Schwarzlose 1908, Bergmann–Bayard.  
- Very rigid lockup.

### Recoil-Boosted  
- Mauser C96 — accelerator lever increases bolt velocity.  
- Seen in many early 20th century locked-breech designs.

---

## ⭐ **VI. Long-Recoil Pistols**

### Frommer Stop & Frommer 1912  
- Barrel and bolt recoil together full stroke → very unusual.  
- Inspired by shotgun long-recoil designs.

---

## ⭐ **VII. Gas-Operated Pistols**

### Desert Eagle  
- Gas piston and rotating bolt like a mini rifle.  
- Not delayed blowback; true gas operation.

### Wildey Survivor  
- Adjustable gas system.

---

## ⭐ **VIII. Exotic / Rare Pistol Operating Systems**

### Blow-Forward  
- Schwarzlose 1908: the barrel moves forward while frame stays still.  
- Ultra rare and mechanically strange.

### Linear-Retard (Boberg XR-Series)  
- Movement of cartridge extraction + barrel cam delays unlocking.

### Gas-Seal (Nagant revolver)  
- Cylinder moves forward to seal chamber for suppressed firing.

# 🔍 **DETAILED ENGINEERING SUBTYPES**

## ⭐ **1. Lockwork Families (Internal Mechanisms)**

### **Colt V-Spring System (Python, Detective Special, SAA ancestors)**  
- Leaf (V) spring powers both hammer & trigger return.  
- Very smooth but delicate → timing can drift.

### **Smith & Wesson Rebound-Slide System**  
- Coil-powered rebound slide resets trigger.  
- Extremely durable and consistent lockwork.

### **Ruger Transfer-Bar System**  
- Coil springs + robust investment-cast parts.  
- Strongest modern DA lockwork; very safe.

### **Webley Top-Break DA**  
- Massive stirrup latch + long hammer strut.  
- Designed for mud, cavalry, battlefield service.

### **Korth & Manurhin Match Actions**  
- Roller bearings, polished geometry, extremely low friction.  
- Highest-performance revolver lockwork ever made.

---

## ⭐ **2. Frame Types (Loading / Extraction Architecture)**

### **Fixed-Frame (Side Gate)**  
- Colt SAA, Ruger Blackhawk.  
- Strongest mechanically; slowest to load.

### **Top-Break**  
- Webley, S&W No.3.  
- Fastest reloads; natural auto-ejector.  
- Weaker frame for high-pressure cartridges.

### **Swing-Out Cylinder (Modern Standard)**  
- All modern duty revolvers (S&W, Colt, Ruger).

### **Break-Action**

- Dual action bars = smoother & more durable (Remington 870, Mossberg 500).  
- Modern pumps use **rotating bolts** (like rifles).  
- Extremely tolerant of varied loads.

---

### **Lever-Action Shotguns**
- Winchester 1887 & 1901: tilting block.  
- Modern cowboy-action favorites; mechanically robust.

---

### **Bolt-Action Shotguns**
- Popular in mid-century American hunting.  
- Slow but simple; can handle slugs accurately.

---

## ⭐ **RECOIL-OPERATED SHOTGUNS**

### **Long-Recoil (Browning Auto-5)**
- Barrel + bolt recoil together ~3–4 inches.  
- Classic humpback A5 profile.  
- Extremely reliable; timing rings tune recoil.

- Entire gun recoils; bolt head compresses internal spring.  
- Fastest-cycling semi-auto shotgun action ever mass-produced.  
- Cleaner than gas systems.

- Rem 1100-type.

- Gas ring around magazine tube; elegant engineering.

# 🔍 **DETAILED ENGINEERING SUBTYPES**

## ⭐ **RECOIL-OPERATED MACHINE GUNS**

### **1. Short-Recoil (Largest Historical Category)**
The barrel and bolt recoil together briefly; bolt unlocks after rearward travel.

**Examples:**
- **MG42 / MG3** — roller-locked, extremely fast (~1200 rpm).  
- **Browning 1919 / M2HB** — tilting breech block.  
- **Maxim / Vickers** — toggle-locked short recoil with heavy water jacket.  
- **St-Etienne 1907** — strange rack & pinion to delay/redefine recoil motion.  
- **Hotchkiss 1914** — flapper-locked.

**Traits:**  
- Very strong lockup.  
- Tunable cyclic rate (MG42 rollers & booster).  
- Good for sustained fire.  
- Often heavy (water jackets, heavy receivers).

---

### **2. Long-Recoil**
Barrel and bolt recoil fully together, then separate.

**Examples:**  
- **Chauchat** LMG  
- Rare MG experiments

**Traits:**  
- Complicated timing.  
- Sensitive to dirt.  
- Very soft recoil impulse (in theory).  

---

## ⭐ **GAS-OPERATED MACHINE GUNS**

### **1. Long-Stroke Gas Piston**
Direct, robust impulse; piston moves full carrier travel.

**Examples:**  
- **PKM** — ultimate reliability, lightweight for GPMG (~17 lbs).  
- **RPD** — belt-fed LMG with fixed barrel.  
- **Bren Gun** — superb accuracy, top-mag feed.  
- **Nambu LMGs, BAR** — early long-stroke designs.

**Traits:**  
- Most reliable MG architecture.  
- Works well with varying ammunition.  
- Smooth impulse.

---

### **2. Short-Stroke Gas Piston**
Piston taps carrier briefly; carrier continues alone.

**Examples:**  
- **M249 SAW / FN Minimi**  
- **Lewis Gun**  
- **FG-42** (hybrid recoil + short-stroke gas)

**Traits:**  
- Lighter reciprocating mass → smoother recoil.  
- Often higher cyclic rates.  
- Easier to control for LMG roles.

---

### **3. Gas Trap (Obsolete)**  
Gas captured at muzzle instead of barrel port.

**Examples:**  
- Early Japanese MGs

**Traits:**  
- Complex, inefficient; quickly abandoned historically.

---

### **4. Gas-Boosted Recoil**
Recoil impulse is augmented by gas pressure to ensure reliability at extreme ROF.

**Examples:**  
- MG30, MG34 developmental lineage  
- Some aircraft MGs

---

## ⭐ **BLOWBACK & DELAYED BLOWBACK MACHINE GUNS**

Mostly pistol-caliber or low-pressure rifle-caliber.

### **1. Roller-Delayed Blowback**
**Examples:**  
- HK21  
- HK23  
- HK variants for 5.56 / 7.62

Traits:
- Clean, consistent unlocking.  
- High accuracy potential.  
- Excellent suppression characteristics.

---

### **2. Flapper-Locked Blowback (DP-28)**
Bolt flaps lock into receiver recesses.

**Traits:**  
- Very reliable.  
- Simple to manufacture.  
- Unique to Degtyaryov designs.

---

### **3. API Blowback (Advanced Primer Ignition)**
Bolt fires cartridge *before* fully closed; extremely high ROF.

Used in:
- Aircraft guns  
- Some early SMG/LMG experiments

---

## ⭐ **EXTERNALLY POWERED MACHINE GUNS**

### **1. Electric Motor Driven ("Minigun" Family)**
- GE M134  
- GAU-17/A  
- Air-cooled, multi-barrel, extremely high ROF (~2000–6000 rpm)

**Traits:**  
- Cannot jam in conventional sense; if motor turns, it fires.  
- Requires power supply.  
- Zero cookoff risk (open bolt behavior via rotary cycling).

---

### **2. Hand-Cranked Gatling Guns**
- Original 1862 Gatling  
- Modern reproductions  
- Mechanically elegant, non-automatic until legally redefined

---

### **3. Hybrid Electric/Hydraulic**
Aircraft-class MGs that use:
- Electric priming  
- Hydraulic ram assist  
- Pneumatic cycling

- **■■■■□** = 4/5  
- **■■■■■** = 5/5  

Each square is a “slot,” and each filled ■ corresponds to 1 point.

---

# 🔧 Why you sometimes only see 4 visible slots (■■■□)?

Formatting compresses it visually:

- It’s still **four visible slots**, but conceptually it represents a 5-slot scale.  
- The last □ implies the fifth slot, even though it’s visually not separated into five blocks.

Or, if you want even more explicit formatting:
