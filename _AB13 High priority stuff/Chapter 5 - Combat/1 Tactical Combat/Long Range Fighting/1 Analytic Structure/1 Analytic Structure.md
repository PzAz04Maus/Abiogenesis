Throughout history, war has been perceived as action on three broad levels: strategic, operational and tactical. Clausewitz distinguished the three levels by relativity in time, space and mass.

If fighting consisted of a single act, no further subdivision would be needed. However, it consists of a greater or lesser number of single acts, each complete in itself, which... are called "engagements". This gives rise to the completely different activity of planning and executing these engagements themselves, and of coordinating each of them with the others in order to further the object of the war. One has been called tactics, and the other strategy... According to our classification, then, tactics teaches the use of armed forces in the engagement; strategy, the use of engagements for the object of the war.[1]

# Version 2: the operations gap

the actions and effects of the modern story can be categorized in three different, but interrelated levels of decisionmaking: tactical, operational, and strategic. 

rpgs spend the most time handling the tactical level of analysis, while they devote less to the strategic level - usually only supporting it with narrative tools. 

But what they do the least is the operational level, the in-between region of tactics and strategy. This region has a lot of potential for interesting developments, such as better ways to manage survival, supply, encounters or scouting if it were handled in a better  coordinated fashion.

For example, maybe it'd be cool to play an open ended Oregon trail campaign with branching paths in the wilderness. Or to extend on the hex crawl.

---

The tactical level consists of the sharp, intense action that arrives at contact with the enemy. They correspond to the scene.

The operational level consists of the actions that contribute to the fight, such as positioning, planning, support, reconnaissance and followthrough.

The strategic level serves to find link these immediate efforts in purpose to the wider objective. - what if this is wrong and strategic actions correspond to the campaign arc, like one book in a trilogy?

All rpgs and all players are highly familiar with the tactical level. However, it is the structure of the operational level that needs to be explored.
