# Fear Country
--

A future had never been so possible before now, Ha-joon thought in the most elated mood he had been in years. The road before him was like a coronial procession of evergreen, bidding him to an opportunity of a lifetime. Just finish this job right and nothing, absolutely nothing nor nobody, would stop him. Not his beat up old car, not any of the ancient wrecks on the road, not a thief or murderer nor one mistake placed in his path.

Free. He rolled the concept around in cupped hands, careful not to let it have the opportunity to escape from his grasp as he glided past an abandoned construction machine on the wrong side of the road, taking care to slow down. While his destination was safe, the only route there was the cut throat corners of a scenic road coursing through the alpine, each blind alley a potential vector for disaster, bandits, or worse.

But what was the price of success except the fear of failure? Ha-joon coolly contended. He had all the stuff to do this, but this opportunity was now.

The next bend felt no different in Ha-joon's mind - until he saw a lump in the middle of the road. He yanked the steering column to the left, dipping the car cleanly through a ragged procession of cars on the side of the road and came to a stop in front of a van stopped across the road, both of its ends crushed by some accident.

His car's headlights shone daylight on the van's side door, the panels threaded through and through with dozens of bullet holes. Ha-joon senselessly took it all in, looking around at the holes, the blood, the carnage right in front of him.

When he turned to his left, his heart sank back down again. People, average people, the kind who would populate a bus station, were shivering in a circle around a small fire, using the remains of a bus that had taken a mine as a break from the cold winter wind.
