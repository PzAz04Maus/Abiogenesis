# magnitudes of decision

The car, the radio, and the rifle create situations where actions of asymmetric magnitude exist in the same encounter. For example, a creature may move 15 meters in a turn, but firearms may have a range of 400 meters, meaning that if an animal runs at a shooter at maximum range, it will take them 27 moves before they can strike. This high ratio represents a worst case scenario for both action economy and play efficiency.

Some of these undesirable results can be avoided through better encounter design. But in the context a modern settings, these asymmetries eventually become unavoidable. 

Yet there is opportunity to explore these asymmetries in gameplay; they just have to be effectively managed.

The operational scale in AB13 builds on the operational pause from TW13. It is a lower frequency, larger scale of decision making that focuses on the efforts that lead to and support the tactical actions, such as the use of vehicles, or when large amounts of terrain must be traversed.

Additionally, the operational scale is used to manage long form tactical encounters such as long range fires or chases. These are called deep encounters.

## operational scene (tactical scene)

An **operational scene** is the collection of all [exchanges of fire](/_AB13%20Low%20priority%20stuff/Chapter%205%20-%20Combat/_Master.md)[^2], pauses, [operational actions](/_AB13%20Low%20priority%20stuff/Chapter%205%20-%20Combat/_Master.md#Operational%20Actions)[^3], or operational movements. These are collectively referred to as **operational decisions**. Operational scenes begin when approaching the enemy.

Unlike tactical exchanges, operational scenes do not have initiative rolls. Each group makes an operational decision, then each turn is resolved simultaneously. Any group member interrupted by an exchange of fire during a non-combat related operational action must give up their initiative check, even if they don't continue their operational action. This does not apply to movement.

To simplify matters, a group may chain multiple operational decisions together and then resolve it as one unit.

Operational decisions range from 20 to 80 seconds in length, but can be simplified to 60. With an **operational move** action, a group may move up to 10 times their best possible tactical speed. This move speed may be increased with an athletics or drive check as applicable.

The only time an attack can be made on the operational scale is during an exchange of fire.

Information about the enemy group is limited during the operational scene; characters only know where the enemy's center is, and are to be given no instructions on enemy intentions other than what they can intuit themselves, unless they make a reconnaissance  check (previously known as the keep watch check). 

The Withdraw operational action is now classified as a [Stage]()[^4] I Rule. For Stage II rules, a group that wishes to disengage has to lose the opponent with obstructions or [intervisible](https://leaderbusiness.blogspot.com/2008/05/intervisibility-lines.html) concealment.

## Contact!

Contact is when the party is interrupted at an unexpected time or place. 

The Contact mechanic bridges the gap between the operational or tactical scale and the individual scale. 

The contact mechanic is all about situational awareness. Situational awareness has 2 dimensions:
- the quality of data that you recognize
- how fast you recognize them.

The speed of recognition determines the standoff between the party and the threat. It is set by an ooda roll made by the closest person to the threat. This is called the pointman. Standoff can be scaled in increments of distance or time

The quality of data determines how well the point man understands the situation. The result is set by a perception roll. 

Data has 2 considerations 
- detection - the presence and location of an object
- recognition - determining the meaning of that data through patterns

 Perceptive magnitude is a mixture of how much evidence of the impending act the character detects and interprets the incident. For example.

## Initial phase, no player shaping
If the two groups do not shape the battle prior to engaging (aka: complete surprise), the GM rolls for luck for each side (2d20L!6), then generates the terrain and  based on the fortune of each group. The groups have no decision in how this terrain nor their assembly is set up beyond what they were immediately doing prior to the interruption.
## Subsequent phases

1. GM determines whether players may add to the map depending on the local Level one terrain surrounding the immediate area in a particular direction
2. The group then rolls a terrain check with a modifier based on the local region the terrain is a part of in one cardinal direction.
	1. on success, the group adds a level zero terrain piece onto the map in an arrangement of their choosing in consultation with the GM. The margin of success determines how well the arrangement should suit them.
	2. On failure, the group adds a level zero piece of terrain onto the map, but the GM determines how the arrangement disadvantages them.
