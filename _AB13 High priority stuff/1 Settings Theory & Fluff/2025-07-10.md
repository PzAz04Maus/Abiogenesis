# de kuiper filament

On the day of the incident, a dense molecular filament one lightyear in width formed through the solar system in a matter of hours, stemming from the direction of the transmission. 

This molecular cloud was formed with a high density of interstellar exotica, unknown matter and energies not  encountered before in the literature. In response, the standard model of particle physics had to be supplemented with one that covered the dark exceptions.

When it achieved a critical length, the filament collapsed inward, into the solar system where it fragmented into clouds and accreted into cores composed of standard model and dark model materials. These cores would sometimes 'ignite,' causing processes that 

- synthesized stable transuranic elements 
- form irregular analogues to nucleosynthesis (a combination of standard and dark material)
- drives transdimensional reactions to produce higher dimensional compounds
- forms matter-force composites (other than photons) such as an electromagnetic pseudopole or a strong nuclear photon
- or produce exotic forces, including gravity
- introduced stable antimatter

Note: dark matter is actually a misnomer. Its original use was to describe the matter needed for the big bang model ((ΛCDM model) to be correct that hasn't been observed. According to theory, This matter wasn't supposed to interact strongly with standard model baryonic matter or energy such as light, but the exotica of the de kuiper event only shows some of the behavior required of dark matter.

In consequence, there is hot debate in renaming the dark matter model into the exotic model or new model to remove inaccuracy. However, it remains colloquially known as dark matter.
