# ✔ How these numbers are calculated (short version)

These absolute limits come from:

## 1. **Textile Tensile Strength**

Typical 500D–1000D Cordura has:

- **Breaking strength:** ~150–200 lbs (67–90 kg) with proper stitching  
    But a pack has _multiple points of failure_, so overall limit is much lower.
    

## 2. **Bar-Tack Stitch Ratings (mil-spec sewing)**

MIL-spec bar-tack stitching (8–12 passes) fails at:

- **300–450 lbs tension** when pulled linearly
    
- But straps distribute load → practical catastrophic point ~50–70 kg.
    

## 3. **Frame Limits**

- ALICE frame: bends at **~50–55 kg**
    
- MOLLE II polymer frame: cracks around **60–65 kg**
    

## 4. **Zipper Test Standards**

ASTM D2061: typical #8–#10 zippers:

- **Failure range:** 90–120 lbs of force (40–55 kg)
    
- Smaller daypacks use #5 zippers → fail around **20–25 kg**
    

## 5. **Internal Volume & Density Constraint**

Even if structurally OK, you must _fit_ the mass.

Max density for "packable solids" like sand/gravel/lead shot gives:

- **20 L pack ≈ 20 kg (at 1 kg/L density)**
    
- **60 L pack ≈ 60 kg**
    
- **100 L pack ≈ 100 kg**
    

This matches structural failure thresholds fairly well.
